Step 1 — the input

Three ordinary segments — drag to rotate. Construction gets substantially into inserting the third one, then fails while trying to locate a curved edge's own flat context.

Step 2 — the failing edge

The orange arc is edge 874 — not a straight line, a genuine curve confined to a quadric, traced between its own two endpoints (orange dots) exactly as the construction itself carries it. find_flat_neighbor_face needs a live, flat, dim=2 face touching this edge to locate it in local 2D coordinates. It scans every face that references edge 874 and finds none.

Step 3 — the surface this edge actually sits on

The faint blue wireframe is bisector(segment 1, segment 2)'s own surface, sampled the same way the construction itself parametrizes it (ruled_frame / ruling_line) — not a mockup surface, the real one. Four live faces touch edge 874: 875, 876, 878, 879. All four carry curve = bisector(segment 1, segment 2) — checked directly, their quadric matrices are bit-identical. This isn't four different surfaces coincidentally meeting at a point; it's one surface, cut into four separate pieces by earlier clips, all of which still touch this one edge. None of the four happens to be flat.

What's actually wrong

A curved dim=1 edge at N=3 doesn't carry its own embedding plane — find_flat_neighbor_face's docstring explains why: "curve lives on facets," so a curved edge needs *some* flat neighboring face to read off local 2D coordinates from. Every curved edge produced by capping a single cut has exactly one such neighbor, by construction. The assumption breaks down after enough accumulated clips: here, four separate cuts have each carved their own piece out of the same original bisector surface, and by pure bad luck all four surviving pieces that still reference edge 874 are themselves curved (cut by yet other bisectors), with no flat piece left touching it anywhere.

This is exactly the case the function's own docstring already named as out of scope ("two different segments' own caps meeting... beyond what either of those callers can handle yet") — not a surprise bug, a known, deliberately-unhandled gap now pinned down to a concrete, minimal repro instead of just a name. Fixing it needs a genuine "locate a curved edge with no flat context at all" primitive — parametrizing directly off the edge's own two defining quadrics rather than borrowing a neighboring face's coordinates — not a small patch.
